# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Alaska locals and visitors planning or actively on outdoor trips — fishing spots, camping sites, gear rentals, and navigation. Used pre-trip on a desktop or mid-trip on a phone. Primary jobs: find a legal fishing water fast, understand what species are present and how to target them, figure out what gear to rent, and navigate to the right spot.

## Product Purpose

Alaska Atlas is a field-grade outdoor guide for Alaska. It gives anglers, campers, and recreational visitors regulation-accurate fishing information, curated field guides by category (fishing, hiking, camping, wildlife, aurora, food), a gear rental catalog through Turnagain Outfitters, and an interactive map of Alaska waters and sites. Success looks like a user opening the app at the boat launch, finding their water in under 30 seconds, knowing what's legal to keep, and walking away with the right gear.

## Brand Personality

Trusted field guide. Authoritative without being bureaucratic. Calm, encyclopedic, confident — like a weathered regulation booklet that actually makes sense, or the knowledge of a guide who's fished every drainage in Southcentral. The UI earns trust through precision, not polish.

Three words: **grounded, authoritative, alive**.

## Anti-references

- **Government reg sites** (ADF&G PDFs): correct data buried in wall-of-text, zero visual trust-building, punishing to navigate. Alaska Atlas is the antidote.
- **Outdoor retail apps** (REI, Bass Pro): shopping-first, merchandise-heavy, cross-sell everywhere. Alaska Atlas is a field tool, not a storefront.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust through precision** — every label, water name, and species data point should be correct and feel correct. Vague copy erodes the field-guide authority.
2. **Field conditions first** — dark background, high-contrast text, large tap targets. This is used at dawn on a riverbank, not in a Starbucks.
3. **Get out of the way fast** — the fastest path to the answer wins. No gatekeeping, no required accounts, no onboarding wizards before the map loads.
4. **Alaska-specific, not generic outdoors** — every design choice should reinforce that this is specifically for Alaska: the palette, the photography, the species, the place names.
5. **Data is the feature** — the fishing regs, species profiles, lake outlines, and gear catalog are the product. The UI is the frame, not the art.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No hard requirements currently set. Practical minimum: WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1 body text), large touch targets for mobile/field use.
